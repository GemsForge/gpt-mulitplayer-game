# Chromalock Story Progression

Chromalock follows two ordinary factory workers through six cooperative shifts. The factory begins in soft grayscale, and each completed level restores color, agency, and visible personality to the workers. The story is communicated through short environmental cues, avatar changes, and the shared objective—not through dense lore panels.

## Level 1 — First Shift: Match the Door

### Objective

Match the room's target color to the exit door by activating the single available color station together. Teach movement, interaction, the shared goal, and the idea that both workers must participate.

### Location

The Orientation Room: a quiet grayscale workroom with one pastel-blue door, one color station, two worker terminals, and a clearly marked floor path. The workers begin with plain gray uniforms, blank badges, and identical neutral avatars.

### Obstacles

- A short path of harmless floor barriers requires both workers to reach separate activation pads.
- The door remains locked until both pads are active at the same time.
- No countdown is used; the level establishes confidence before introducing pressure.

**Player-facing progression cues:** The shared objective changes from `MATCH THE DOOR` to `READY` when both pads are occupied. On success, the door opens, the workers' badges gain a soft powder-blue edge, and the message `SHIFT COMPLETE · COLOR RESTORED: 1` appears.

**New gameplay idea:** Two-player simultaneous activation.

## Level 2 — Production Error: Mix the Signal

### Objective

Repair a factory color mixer by combining two primary color inputs to produce the pastel target shown on the repair door. Players learn that the desired color can require contributions from separate stations.

### Location

The Mixing Room: a clean, pale-cream chamber with two separated input stations, a central mixer, and an exit marked with a mint target swatch plus a matching pattern icon. The workers receive their first visible uniform upgrade: a colored sleeve matching the station they operate.

### Obstacles

- Each worker can operate only one color station at a time.
- The mixer rejects an incomplete combination and gives a clear `MISSING INPUT` message.
- An incorrect combination resets the current mix, not the entire level.

**Player-facing progression cues:** The objective lists `1. BRING BLUE`, `2. BRING YELLOW`, `3. MIX`, with completed steps receiving a checkmark and a pattern change. On success, the mixer blooms mint, the workers gain colored gloves, and `YOU MADE A NEW COLOR TOGETHER` appears.

**New gameplay idea:** Cooperative color mixing.

## Level 3 — Restricted Access: Split Knowledge

### Objective

Open the Restricted Access door by following a color sequence. Each player sees one half of the sequence, so they must communicate their clue and activate the stations in the correct order.

### Location

The Restricted Access Hall: a narrow, uncluttered corridor with two color stations on opposite sides and a door displaying three large symbol-color slots. Each worker's avatar gains a small personal accent: one has a peach badge, the other a lavender badge.

### Obstacles

- Player-specific clue panels hide one part of the sequence from the other player.
- A locked station cannot be used until the partner completes the preceding step.
- Activating the wrong station resets only the active sequence step and briefly displays the reason.

**Player-facing progression cues:** The shared panel shows `SEQUENCE 0/3`, then fills one numbered slot at a time. A partner ping (`Q`) places a visible marker and the text `I FOUND MY CLUE` in the activity feed. On success, both workers receive a new ID badge and the factory lights briefly reveal their faces.

**New gameplay idea:** Asymmetric information and player communication.

## Level 4 — Fading Instructions: Remember the Shift

### Objective

Use a temporary text clue to determine the order in which warm and cool color stations must be activated, then match the final target on the exit door.

### Location

The Archive Floor: a soft lavender records room with three large stations, a central message display, and a door whose color target is shown with both a name and a pattern. The workers gain a visible hair, hat, or tool accessory selected by their cooperation score.

### Obstacles

- The text clue appears for **10 seconds**, with a visible `CLUE ACTIVE` countdown, then fades.
- The clue must be remembered and communicated; it is not repeated unless the team deliberately spends a reset token.
- A rotating filter temporarily changes the appearance of the stations, but their labels and patterns remain available.

Example timed clue: `WARM FIRST. COOL SECOND. THE PALE SIGNAL OPENS THE DOOR.`

**Player-facing progression cues:** The clue timer uses a progress bar and a plain-language warning at five seconds: `SAY THE CLUE BEFORE IT FADES`. After the clue expires, the shared panel displays `MEMORY SEQUENCE · 0/3`. On success, both avatars gain a soft lavender glow and a `MEMORY RECORDED` stamp appears.

**New gameplay idea:** Timed text clues and cooperative memory.

## Level 5 — Emergency Override: Read Beyond Color

### Objective

Restore the emergency system while the factory's color system is offline. Use symbols, names, patterns, and communicated observations to identify the correct sequence without relying on color alone.

### Location

The Emergency Control Room: a mostly grayscale room with muted pastel outlines, high-contrast labels, geometric patterns, and a large shared emergency console. The workers' uniforms now show their accumulated accent colors, but the room deliberately suppresses most color.

### Obstacles

- A temporary blackout removes color fills for **15 seconds** after each step.
- False-color panels use a tempting appearance but carry the wrong symbol or text label.
- Conveyor belts move workers between marked stations, making timing and location callouts important.
- The room provides an explicit accessibility reminder: `COLOR IS A CLUE, NOT THE ONLY CLUE`.

**Player-facing progression cues:** The shared objective names every target using color, shape, and pattern: `MINT · DOTS · SAFE`. Correct actions restore one room accent; incorrect actions produce a readable `SYMBOL DOES NOT MATCH` message. On success, the factory's lights return and each worker gains a personal Spark outline.

**New gameplay idea:** Multimodal clues and resilience when color information is unavailable.

## Level 6 — Find Your Spark: Open the Factory

### Objective

Create the final Spark by combining the workers' learned color signals in the correct cooperative order. Open the factory's main door and choose the expression each worker wants to carry into the world.

### Location

The Heart of the Factory: a large, uncluttered circular chamber with two personal color wells, a shared Spark Core, and the sealed main door. The factory is no longer gray; it is a calm pastel gradient built from the players' completed colors. The workers stand in upgraded uniforms with visible faces, badges, accessories, and individual aura accents.

### Obstacles

- The Spark Core requires both players to contribute different signals; either player acting alone produces no progress.
- A final text clue appears for **12 seconds** and describes the order indirectly: `Begin with what calms, add what wakes, then choose what is yours.`
- The Core changes its target pattern after each correct contribution, requiring players to compare the shared objective with their personal wells.
- A mistake returns the Core to the previous step, preserving earned level progress while making the consequence understandable.

**Player-facing progression cues:** The shared panel shows `SPARK 0/3`, `SPARK 1/3`, and `SPARK COMPLETE`. Each contribution changes the workers' avatars immediately: gray uniform, then restored palette, then personal Spark color and animated outline. When the door opens, the final message reads `THE FACTORY ASSIGNED YOUR JOBS. YOU FOUND YOUR SPARKS.`

**New gameplay idea:** A cooperative finale that combines every learned mechanic into an expressive identity choice.

## Progression rules

- Each level teaches exactly one new gameplay idea; previous ideas remain available as supporting skills.
- The shared objective is always visible, short, numbered when sequencing is required, and paired with text plus non-color visual cues.
- Timed clues use a readable countdown, announce when they are fading, and never hide the only information needed to continue.
- Failure resets the current puzzle step rather than the entire level unless the team explicitly chooses a full reset.
- Every completed level changes at least one visible part of the workers or factory, so story progression is observable without a lore dump.
- The final Spark is earned through cooperation, not accumulated by one player.
